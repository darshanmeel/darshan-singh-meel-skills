# Klassenstufen 11-13

Paper: 30 problems, 75 min, start 30, maximum 150 (`wettbewerb.md`). One paper for three school years. Problem families and limits below are this skill's working rules; the organiser publishes no syllabus.

## Curriculum
- check: KCGO Hessen, Mathematik (kultus.hessen.de → Kerncurricula → Gymnasiale Oberstufe) - not read for this skill. Take Kompetenzbereich, Bildungsstandard codes and Themenfelder from the Oberstufe reference of `darshan-singh-meel-schule-mathe` or from the PDF, never from memory.
- Until then the material cites `KCGO Hessen, Mathematik` without Kompetenzbereich or Bildungsstandard code; the gap is named in the reply, never as a `check:` note in the file.
- Safe base for content: everything in the Sek I KC up to the end of Jg. 9/10 (`klassen-09-10.md`).

## Knowledge that may be used
| Use | Content |
|---|---|
| Free | all Sek I content: algebra, functions (linear, quadratic, power, exponential, sine), geometry incl. trigonometry and similarity, probability with tree diagrams |
| In 4- and 5-point problems, defined in the stem when not standard | properties of functions and graphs (monotony, symmetry, zeros), sequences, inequalities, absolute value, logarithm as inverse of the power, combinatorics (factorial, choosing k of n), number theory (primes, remainders) |
| Only when the user confirms the course has had it | derivatives and integrals, vectors and analytic geometry, binomial distribution |

- Jahrgang 11 (Einführungsphase) sits the same paper as the Qualifikationsphase: a problem that needs Q-phase content is unfair to part of the group. Default: none.
- No calculator and no CAS: exact values, small numbers, expressions that collapse.

## Problem families
| Points | Families |
|---|---|
| 3 | value of an expression with powers, roots or logarithms that collapses · which graph belongs to which function · number of solutions of an equation · simple probability · area or angle in a standard figure · negation of a statement |
| 4 | functional reasoning (f(f(x)), symmetry, periodicity) · equation with a parameter: for how many values … · geometry with circles and similar triangles · counting with restrictions · divisibility of expressions in n · sequences defined by a rule · expected value in a small game |
| 5 | extremal problems by inequality or symmetry · number theory with remainders and factorisations · solids: sections and shortest paths on surfaces · invariants and colourings · polynomials: coefficients and zeros · probability with a clever count · statements about all or some functions with a property |

## Language
- Mathematical language as in the Oberstufe: quantifiers explicit (`für alle`, `es gibt`), domains stated, symbols defined in the stem.
- Options are often expressions; all five in the same form (simplified, same variable).

## Typical mistakes to build distractors from
- dividing an equation by an expression that can be zero
- squaring that adds solutions; domain of root and logarithm ignored
- "for all" read as "there is"
- conditional probability reversed
- overcounting by order; forgetting symmetric cases
- inequality direction after multiplying by a negative number
