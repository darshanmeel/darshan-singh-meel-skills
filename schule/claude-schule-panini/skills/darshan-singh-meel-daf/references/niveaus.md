# Levels A1-C1 (GER / CEFR)

Framework: Gemeinsamer europäischer Referenzrahmen für Sprachen (GER), Council of Europe 2001, with the Companion Volume / Begleitband 2020 (extended descriptors; adds mediation, online interaction, plurilingual and pluricultural competence). Status of each fact: `sources.md`.

- Never print a descriptor as a quotation unless it was read in the Companion Volume: check: `rm.coe.int` → "CEFR Companion volume with new descriptors 2020" (German: Begleitband, Klett 2020). The lines below are our own summaries.
- Bands: A = elementare Sprachverwendung (Basic User) · B = selbstständige (Independent User) · C = kompetente (Proficient User).

## What learners can do (own summary, sinngemäß)
| Level | Verstehen (Hören, Lesen) | Sprechen, Interaktion | Schreiben | Mediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | familiar words and very simple sentences when spoken slowly; names, signs, short notices, forms | introduce oneself, ask and answer simple questions about person, place, things; needs a helpful partner | fill in a form; a short simple message or greeting | pass on one simple piece of information (time, place, price) |
| A2 | frequent expressions on person, family, shopping, work, surroundings; short everyday texts, simple e-mails, ads, timetables | routine exchanges; describe background, education, job in simple terms; short social contacts | short notes, messages and simple personal e-mails | relay the main point of a short, clear message or announcement |
| B1 | main points of clear standard speech on work, school, leisure; straightforward factual texts, personal letters | cope with most travel and everyday situations; tell experiences, give short reasons and plans | connected simple text on familiar topics; personal letters with experiences and impressions | pass on the content of a simple text or conversation; take simple notes for others |
| B2 | main ideas of complex texts on concrete and abstract topics, specialist discussion in the own field; longer talks, most news | spontaneous, fluent enough for normal talk with native speakers; explain a viewpoint with pros and cons | clear, detailed text on many topics; essay or report with arguments | summarise and pass on detailed information and arguments from longer texts |
| C1 | demanding longer texts, implicit meaning; long talks even when not clearly structured | fluent and spontaneous; flexible use in social, academic and professional life | clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex matters; controlled use of connectors and structure | convey complex content precisely for a given audience, mark nuances |

## Working ranges per level (didactic defaults, not official numbers)
| Level | Reading text | Sentence | Writing task | Speaking turn | Vocabulary known (whole level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 30-80 words | up to 8 words, main clauses, `und/oder/aber` | 20-40 words, 3 content points | 1-2 sentences | about 650 entries (Goethe A1 word list: read) |
| A2 | 80-150 | up to 12 words; `weil`, `dass`, `wenn` | 40-80 words | 3-5 sentences | about 1 300 lexical units (Goethe A2 word list: read in a search excerpt of the PDF) |
| B1 | 150-300 | up to 18 words; subordinate and relative clauses | 80-120 words | 1-2 min | about 2 400 lexical units (Goethe B1 word list: read) |
| B2 | 300-500 | free; nominal style appears | 150-200 words | 3-4 min | no official list |
| C1 | 500-800 | free; complex syntax | 200-350 words | 5-7 min | no official list |

- Half levels inside a course (A1.1, A1.2 ...): first half = first half of the level's grammar in `grammatik.md`; say which half the material targets.
- The reading range applies to the main reading text of a lesson or test part. Short text types (Anzeige, Schild, Notiz, Formular, SMS) are exempt at every level; ±10 % is tolerated; a text for a writing or mediation task may be shorter.
- A text is at level when a learner of that level knows about 95 % of the words; the rest goes into the glossary (at most 8 words per text).

## How much English (setup question 4)
| Level | English line on | German only (`data-nur-de`) |
|---|---|---|
| A1, A2 | every instruction, task wording, rule explanation, Merksatz, typical-mistake note, hint; glossary | texts, dialogues, example sentences, gap texts, tables of forms, Redemittel, model answers |
| B1 | instructions, rule explanations, typical-mistake notes; glossary | as above, plus Merksätze written at level |
| B2, C1 | instructions (short form) and glossary only | everything else; `body data-sprache="de"` when the user chooses glossary only |
| Teacher pages, all levels (Stundenentwurf, Erwartungshorizont, Bewertungsraster, notes in the Lösung) | every line | quoted target language |

- Instructions are written at the learners' level too: A1 `Lesen Sie.` `Kreuzen Sie an.`; the English line helps, it does not excuse hard German.
- Contrast with English only where it prevents a known error (word order, `since`/`seit`, false friends); one line, in the typical-mistake box.
