# Classic studies: fact rows

Use these rows for study summaries, tasks and solutions. Status: written from subject knowledge, **not compared with the primary publications in this skill's build** (`sources.md`). When a graded task depends on an exact figure, verify it in the primary publication (column "Publication") and add `Quelle:` under the material. A study not listed here → primary source, or `check:`.

Study summaries in material are written for the task (120-250 words), never copied from a textbook. Name the publication in the source line: `nach: Asch (1956)`.

## Gedächtnis, Denken (E2.1)
| Study | Publication | Sample, design | Result | Limits, criticism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vergessenskurve | Ebbinghaus 1885, "Über das Gedächtnis" | self-experiment (n = 1); lists of sinnlose Silben; Ersparnismethode (time saved when relearning) | saving falls fast, then slowly: about 58 % after 20 min, 44 % after 1 h, 34 % after 1 day, 25 % after 6 days, 21 % after 31 days | one person; meaningless material - meaningful material is forgotten more slowly; the curve shows saving, not "percent remembered" |
| Gedächtnisspanne | Miller 1956, "The magical number seven, plus or minus two" | review article, no single experiment | span of immediate memory about 7 ± 2 units; chunking enlarges the unit | newer estimate about 4 chunks when rehearsal is prevented (Cowan 2001) |
| Dauer des Kurzzeitgedächtnisses | Peterson & Peterson 1959 | 24 students; consonant trigram, then counting backwards in threes for 3-18 s (prevents rehearsal) | correct recall about 80 % after 3 s, under 10 % after 18 s | artificial material; loss may be interference from earlier trials, not only decay |
| Sensorisches Register (ikonisch) | Sperling 1960 | 3 × 4 letter matrix shown for 50 ms; whole report vs. partial report (tone marks one row) | whole report about 4-5 of 12 letters; partial report about 3 of 4 in the cued row → about 9 letters available; advantage gone after about 1 s | few, trained participants; visual store only |
| Mehrspeichermodell | Atkinson & Shiffrin 1968 | theory chapter, no single experiment | three stores: sensorisches Register → Kurzzeitspeicher → Langzeitspeicher; control processes (rehearsal) | see `modelle-animationen.md` |
| Arbeitsgedächtnis | Baddeley & Hitch 1974; Baddeley 2000 (episodischer Puffer) | dual-task experiments | short-term memory has parts that work side by side: zentrale Exekutive, phonologische Schleife, visuell-räumlicher Notizblock | central executive loosely defined |
| Verarbeitungstiefe | Craik & Lockhart 1972 (framework); Craik & Tulving 1975 (experiments) | words judged by look, sound or meaning; later recognition test | deeper (semantic) processing → better retention | "depth" hard to measure independently (circular) |
| Falschinformationseffekt | Loftus & Palmer 1974 | Exp. 1: 45 students watch films of car accidents; question with verb "smashed / collided / bumped / hit / contacted". Exp. 2: 150 students, question about broken glass one week later | speed estimate rises with the verb (smashed about 41 mph, contacted about 32 mph); Exp. 2: "broken glass" reported by 16 of 50 (smashed), 7 of 50 (hit), 6 of 50 (control) - there was none | students, film instead of a real event, no stress; estimate may be response bias in Exp. 1 |

## Lernen (E1.3, E2.4, Q2.1)
| Study | Publication | Sample, design | Result | Limits, criticism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Klassische Konditionierung | Pavlov, lectures published 1927 (English "Conditioned Reflexes") | dogs; neutral stimulus (e.g. metronome, bell) paired with food; saliva measured | after pairing, the former neutral stimulus alone elicits salivation; extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalisation, discrimination | animals; reflexes only |
| "Little Albert" | Watson & Rayner 1920 | one infant (about 9 months at the baseline test, 11 months at conditioning); white rat paired with a loud noise | fear reaction to the rat, reported to spread to similar objects | n = 1, no control, loose measurement; fear was not removed; no consent by today's standards; the child's identity is disputed |
| Operante Konditionierung | Skinner 1938, "The Behavior of Organisms" | rats and pigeons in the Skinner-Box; lever press followed by food or by the end of a shock | behaviour followed by reinforcement becomes more frequent; schedules of reinforcement change the resistance to extinction | animals; cognition left out |
| Lernen am Modell ("Bobo doll") | Bandura, Ross & Ross 1961 | 72 children (36 girls, 36 boys), about 3-6 years; aggressive model · non-aggressive model · no model (24 each); then observation in a play room | children with the aggressive model showed clearly more imitated aggression; boys more physical aggression | laboratory, a doll made to be hit, short-term effect; ethics: aggression shown to children |
| Stellvertretende Verstärkung | Bandura 1965 | 66 children; film model rewarded · punished · no consequence | less imitation after the punished model; with an incentive all groups could reproduce the acts → acquisition ≠ performance | as above |

## Entwicklung (Q1)
| Study | Publication | Sample, design | Result | Limits, criticism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invarianz (Umschüttversuch), Drei-Berge-Versuch | Piaget; Piaget & Inhelder 1956 (three mountains) | clinical interviews with children | under about 7 years: quantity judged by one dimension; own view chosen for the doll → Egozentrismus | small samples, task language; simpler tasks show earlier competence: McGarrigle & Donaldson 1974 ("naughty teddy"), Hughes 1975 (policeman task), Baillargeon 1987 (object permanence at about 4 months) |
| Moralische Entwicklung | Kohlberg 1958 (dissertation) and follow-up studies | 72 boys aged 10-16, Chicago; dilemmas (Heinz-Dilemma); the reasoning counts, not the decision | three levels, six stages | boys only; judgement ≠ action; culture; Gilligan 1982: ethics of care |
| Pygmalion-Effekt (Rosenthal-Effekt, Q3.4) | Rosenthal & Jacobson 1968 | one primary school, grades 1-6; teachers told that about 20 % (randomly chosen) would "bloom"; IQ test after 8 months | larger IQ gains for the named children, clear only in grades 1 and 2 | test unsuited for the youngest; effect in two grades only; later reviews find small effects (Jussim & Harber 2005) |

## Sozialpsychologie (Q3)
| Study | Publication | Sample, design | Result | Limits, criticism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Konformität | Asch 1951; 1956 | male US college students; one real participant in a group of confederates (check: Asch 1956 for the group size; usually given as 7-9 persons in all); line comparison; 18 trials, 12 critical (confederates agree on a wrong answer); 1956: 123 real participants; control group judging alone | 36.8 % of the answers in critical trials followed the wrong majority; about three quarters conformed at least once, about one quarter never; control: errors under 1 % | young men, USA, 1950s; trivial task among strangers; conformity differs by culture and period (Bond & Smith 1996); Perrin & Spencer 1980 found almost none in a UK sample; deception |
| Gehorsam | Milgram 1963 (first study); 1974 (variations) | 40 men aged 20-50, New Haven; "teacher" told to give a "learner" (confederate) shocks from 15 V to 450 V in 15 V steps; no real shocks | 26 of 40 (65 %) went to 450 V; all 40 went to 300 V; obedience fell with closeness to the learner and with a less prestigious setting | deception, severe stress, pressure to continue (Baumrind 1964); archive work questions how standardised the procedure was and how many believed the shocks (Perry 2013); partial replication stopping at 150 V: Burger 2009 |
| "Stanford Prison Experiment" | Haney, Banks & Zimbardo 1973 | 24 male students chosen from about 75 volunteers; random assignment to guard or prisoner; mock prison; planned 2 weeks | stopped after 6 days; humiliation by some guards, breakdowns of some prisoners | no control group, no fixed hypotheses - a demonstration more than an experiment; Zimbardo acted as prison superintendent; guards were briefed (Le Texier 2019); demand characteristics; self-selection (Carnahan & McFarland 2007); BBC Prison Study found no automatic role adoption (Reicher & Haslam 2006); harm to participants |
| Führungsstile | Lewin, Lippitt & White 1939 | boys' clubs (10-11 years); adult leaders acting autoritär · demokratisch · laissez-faire in rotation | democratic: more cooperation and work without the leader; authoritarian: more output while watched, more aggression or apathy; laissez-faire: least output | boys, USA, 1930s; leaders played roles; value-laden terms |
| Kognitive Dissonanz | Festinger & Carlsmith 1959 | 71 male students; boring task; paid 1 $ or 20 $ to tell the next person it was interesting; control | 1-$ group rated the task as more enjoyable than the 20-$ group and the control | deception; alternative explanation: self-perception theory (Bem 1967) |
| Verantwortungsdiffusion | Darley & Latané 1968 | 72 students; discussion over intercom; a staged seizure; believed number of other listeners varied | helped before the seizure ended: 85 % when alone, 31 % when four others were believed present | staged emergency, deception, students |

## Theories without a single founding study (name them as theories)
| Theory | Publication | Label in material |
|---|---|---|
| Drei-Instanzen-Modell, Abwehrmechanismen | S. Freud 1923, "Das Ich und das Es"; A. Freud 1936 | clinical case observation; core concepts hard to test; historically influential |
| Psychosoziale Entwicklung, 8 Stufen | Erikson 1950 | descriptive stage theory; culture- and time-bound |
| Bedürfnishierarchie | Maslow 1943 | the pyramid picture is not Maslow's own; order not confirmed empirically |
| Personenzentrierte Theorie | Rogers 1959 | Menschenbild-based; concepts hard to operationalise |
| Transaktionales Stressmodell | Lazarus & Folkman 1984 | appraisals overlap in practice and are hard to measure separately |
| Risiko-Wahl-Modell | Atkinson 1957 | tendency = motive × probability × incentive; explains task choice of success- vs. failure-oriented persons |
| Attribution von Erfolg und Misserfolg | Weiner 1971; 1985 | dimensions: Lokation, Stabilität, Kontrollierbarkeit |
| Fünf-Faktoren-Modell | Costa & McCrae 1992 (NEO-FFI, 60 items); German version Borkenau & Ostendorf | lexical, factor-analytic; describes, does not explain; self-report |
| Trauerphasen | Kübler-Ross 1969 | from interviews; a fixed sequence is not confirmed |
| Spiegelneuronen | di Pellegrino et al. 1992 (macaques) | evidence in humans mostly indirect; link to empathy contested |
| Gruppenphasen | Bernstein & Lowy (German edition 1969) | five phases: Orientierung, Machtkampf, Vertrautheit, Differenzierung, Ablösung; from social group work, not experimental |
